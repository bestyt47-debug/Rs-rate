how the roast is generated

The roast engine looks at the same structural signals as the regular rating — number usage, word patterns, length, repeated characters — but instead of turning that into a score, it picks from a bank of commentary lines matched to whatever's actually going on in your username.

A username that's just a word plus a birth year gets roasted differently than one that's all underscores, which gets roasted differently than one that's trying a bit too hard to sound edgy. The goal is commentary that actually reacts to your specific username, not a generic insult copy-pasted for everyone.

is it actually mean?

It's meant to sting a little, not actually hurt — "respectfully" is doing real work in the name. If you want the constructive version instead of the comedy version, the main Rate My Username tool gives you the same underlying analysis as an actual score with a category breakdown instead of a punchline.

Nothing you type is stored or shared anywhere — it's processed to generate the roast and that's it, so feel free to put in whatever username you're self-conscious about without worrying it'll show up somewhere else.

frequently asked questions

Can I roast someone else's username, not just mine?
Yes — the tool doesn't check who the username belongs to, so it works fine for roasting a friend's handle (with their permission, ideally).

Will I get the same roast every time for the same username?
You might get variation between visits — the roast picks from a pool of matching lines rather than one fixed response per username, so trying the same handle twice can surface a different angle.

How is this different from the regular rating?
Same underlying analysis of your username's structure, different output — Rate My Username gives you a 0–100 score and category breakdown; this gives you a one-line reaction instead.
