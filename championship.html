who's the actual @ champion?

Ultimate Username Championship runs a 4-username bracket — two first-round battles, then a final — so instead of one winner you get a tournament.

no sign-ups. enter four usernames, watch them fight it out.

made for fun. judged by questionable science. 💀

how the bracket works

Round one is two separate head-to-head matchups, each scored the same way as the Compare tool — vibe, originality, aura, and memorability. The two winners then face off in a final round to crown one username the champion.

This format matters when you've got more than two options on the table and comparing them one pair at a time would take forever. A bracket forces every username through the same gauntlet and surfaces the strongest one without you having to manually track four separate scores.

good ways to use this

If you're renaming an account and have a shortlist of three or four options, run them all through the bracket instead of agonizing over each one individually. Seeing them get eliminated one by one tends to make the decision feel more final than just reading four separate scores would.

It also works as a group activity — throw in usernames from a few friends and let the bracket settle who's actually got the best handle, instead of everyone just insisting their own is the best.

frequently asked questions

Can I enter fewer than four usernames?
The bracket is built for four — two first-round matchups feeding into a final. If you only have two usernames to compare, the Compare tool is the more direct option.

Does seed order (who's matched against who) affect the outcome?
No — each matchup is scored independently on the usernames themselves, not on bracket position, so it doesn't matter which slot you enter a username into.

Can I save or share the final bracket result?
Yes — once the champion is decided, you can download the result or share it directly from the bracket screen.
